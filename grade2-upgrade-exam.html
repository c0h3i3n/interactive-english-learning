Part 1 · Reading Multiple Choice

A New Friend at School

On Monday, Lily felt shy on her first day at a new school. She stood by the door and held her lunch bag. A girl named Emma smiled and showed her the reading corner. During art time, the children made a small quilt picture. Lily drew a bright sun, and Emma helped her choose blue and green cloth. At recess, they played together. Lily was still a little nervous, but she felt happy because she had a new friend.

1. Why did Lily feel shy?

2. Who showed Lily the reading corner?

3. What did the children make?

4. How did Lily feel at the end?

Example: When I was little, I didn't know how to swim, but now I know how to swim.

The Lemonade Helpers

On Saturday morning, Mia and her friends cleaned their clubhouse. The children wanted to buy new chairs, so they made a lemonade stand. Mia squeezed the lemons. Ben counted the cups, and Sara made a small sign. In the afternoon, many neighbors came by. The children sold twenty-four cups of lemonade. They were tired, but they felt proud because they worked together.

1. Why did the children make a lemonade stand?

2. Who counted the cups?

3. How many cups did they sell?

4. How did the children feel?

Part 1 · Reading Multiple Choice

A Story With a Surprise

Arthur wanted to write a story for his class. He chose a small bird and a hungry tiger as his characters. First, he read about animals in the library. Then he wrote a beginning in which the tiger looked for food. Arthur did not like his first ending, so he changed it. In the new ending, the bird used a clever plan and helped the other animals stay safe. Arthur read the story to his friends. They smiled and asked him to write another story.

1. What did Arthur want to write?

2. Where did Arthur read about animals?

3. Why did Arthur change the ending?

4. What did Arthur’s friends do?
